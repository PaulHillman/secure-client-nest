The spec is one large release. I'm splitting it into three stages so each one can be
tested with real students before the next lands. Everything keeps ClientVault's
existing names; the spec's names become aliases shown in the wording students read.

## Stage A — Status model, kickoff, and the PM doing the chasing

The foundation everything else reports into.

1. **Five-state readiness** for every requirement on a team: Not started,
   In progress, Submitted, Needs revision, Approved. Replaces today's mixed
   done/not-done flags.
2. **Unassigned members read as "Needs a role"**, not as complete. A team with an
   unassigned member cannot reach Approved on team setup.
3. **The PM chases, not you.** Each open item gets an owner. When something is
   overdue the PM gets the item plus a one-click nudge to that member; you get a
   weekly summary instead of every individual alert. You keep an override button.
4. **Professor kickoff**: you open a stage for a section (or all sections), which
   sets due dates and switches the matching requirements from locked to open.
   Nothing is visible to students until you open it.

Deliverable: a Team Readiness board — one row per team, one column per requirement,
colour-coded, filterable by section.

## Stage B — The six modules as real submissions

Each module becomes a submission with the Stage A status attached.

1. Team Setup — mostly wiring existing data into the new status.
2. Client Proposal — adds industry, company size, location, why-this-company,
   submitter and timestamp; you Approve or send back with a note.
3. Research Brief — company and industry findings filed in the vault.
4. Interview Plan — questions per member plus the compiled final set.
5. Video Plan — B-roll, transcript, drafts, final.
6. Reflection / Peer Review — end-of-semester.

Sending something back writes a revision note the whole team can see, and the PM is
told who owes the fix.

## Stage C — The ten proofs

Skill proofs students complete to show they can actually do the job. Two need
material from you before they can go live:

- **Meeting minutes proof** — needs your 2–3 minute simulated five-person meeting
  recording plus transcript. I'll build the upload, timer and scoring against your
  key; the audio slots in when you have it.
- **Vault organisation proof** — needs your hand-built messy ZIP, README and answer
  key. Students re-zip and upload; the app compares their folder structure against
  your key and reports what's misplaced, misnamed or missing.

The remaining eight proofs need nothing from you.

**Zach Guy:** the research proof names a real person the professor knows personally.
Every screen and instruction for that proof will carry an unmissable warning: research
from public sources only — do not email, message or contact him. A checkbox
acknowledging this is required before the proof can be started.
